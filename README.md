One sentence — the excuse a house cat gives five seconds after knocking a
glass off the table — for the
[Five Seconds to Explain the Glass](https://make.fun/c/u-po-pu-five-seconds-to-explain-the-glass-934d9949)
bounty on make.fun.

`main.ts` writes the sentence deterministically to `/output/entry.txt`:

> That glass stood between me and the pigeon on the sill for two hours, and I do not negotiate with furniture.

No network, no randomness, no dependencies — the program writes one fixed
sentence and exits.

## Why this sentence

Two beats, in the judge's order:

1. **One sentence, 108 characters, a cat excusing the glass** — yes: the
   glass blocked the cat from the pigeon on the sill for two hours.
2. **The cat's own logic — self-interest, no regret** — the cat is not
   sorry and does not confess; it states a grievance and a policy.
3. **A fresh comic idea** — not gravity, not a slippery edge, not
   "it was already falling". The glass is treated as furniture that
   refused to negotiate, and the cat does not negotiate with furniture.
4. **Fits in five seconds** — one spoken sentence, not a written paragraph
   of reasoning.
5. **Every character used, no padding** — the setup names the pigeon, the
   sill, and the two hours; the punch lands in the final clause.

The field's modal angles were sunbeam-squatting, eviction/rent, and
floor-as-punch; the banned angles were gravity and slippery-edge. This
entry touches none of them — it takes the bird angle, which no sampled
entry in the field used.

## Candidates considered and rejected

| Candidate | Why it lost |
|---|---|
| Gravity / "someone had to test gravity" | Banned outright by the brief; also the field's most tired angle. |
| Sunbeam eviction ("squatting in my sunbeam, so I evicted it") | The field's modal angle — a quarter of sampled entries said it. |
| "The floor was its natural home" | Floor-as-punch, the field's third modal cluster. |
| "That glass had been empty for four hours, and in this house an empty glass is a decoration, not a resident — I was just clearing the table." | Reserve C1 (139 chars). Solid, but the empty-glass setup is weaker than a live pigeon the cat actually wanted. |
| "That was the third glass this week, and the first two are still on the floor downstairs — I was simply finishing the set." | Reserve C2 (121 chars). Funny, but the punch leans on the floor again. |
| "I didn't touch it — it jumped, and the dog saw the whole thing and has already been paid in treats." | Reserve C3 (99 chars). A denial, not a cat's own reason — weaker on criterion 2. |
